# Te Manawa — Research Brief

Deliberately short. This is a visual-first installation; the aim is that the
motion on screen is *true enough* to the science, not that a visitor reads facts.
Everything here is a hook for the four buttons and the timeline — detail lives in
the sources, not on the screen.

## The one takeaway (the whole point)

**The river is older than the mountains.** The Manawatū carved its gorge by
outpacing the rising ranges, while ice ages and eruptions repeatedly reset the
forest — and moa lived through all of it.

## Deep-time anchors (≈345 ka → <50 ka window)

- **~349 ka — Whakamaru caldera supereruption.** One of the largest Quaternary
  eruptions on Earth; its Rangitawa Tephra blanketed the southern North Island as
  a single fingerprinted marker bed. This is the **reset** point (Button 4).
- **Continuous range uplift + antecedent incision.** The Ruahine–Tararua greywacke
  block rises across the Manawatū's course; most uplift is young (bulk within the
  last ~1 Myr, probably accelerating), so across this whole window the gorge is a
  live *race* between rock-uplift and river-incision. The river holds its course
  (antecedence is the favoured reading; superimposition is the alternative).
- **Milankovitch glacial–interglacial cycling.** The neighbouring Whanganui Basin
  holds a world-class sea-level record. Warm highstands (MIS 11, 9, **5e ~125 ka**)
  flood back and cut marine terraces; cold lowstands drop sea level ~120 m and the
  shoreline retreats far into the South Taranaki Bight. Each beat rebuilds the coast.
- **A sand machine.** During/after each glacial, shelf sand is driven inland as
  successive **dunefields** that dam drainage and pond **wetlands / dune lakes**
  (the Horowhenua pattern). Inland: gravel terraces aggrade in cold stages, incise
  in warm ones.
- **~25.5 ka — Ōruanui (Kawakawa) eruption.** Taupō's VEI-8 event, the largest
  globally in the last ~70 kyr; its KOT ash fell over most of NZ. Optional closing
  marker if the run extends past ~50 ka.

## The living world (the part the sim shows)

Each cycle reshuffles habitat: **glacial** lowlands are comparatively open
(shrubland, grassland, beech) and moa-friendly; **interglacials** bring dense
podocarp–broadleaf forest. Moa populations expand, contract and shift with every
beat — exactly the rise-and-fall the ambient sim renders (no score, just balance).

**Moa (North Island fauna of the period, relatives of the modern four):**
*Euryapteryx curtus* (coastal/stout-legged), *Anomalopteryx didiformis* (little
bush moa), *Pachyornis geranoides* (Mantell's), and probably *Dinornis
novaezealandiae* (NI giant). Set <350 ka these are close relatives/ancestral
populations — treat species IDs as provisional.

**Apex predator — Eyles' harrier (*Circus teauteensis*, kērangi).** The North
Island's large extinct raptor (Haast's eagle is essentially South-Island-only).
Island-giant, ~2 m wingspan, ~3 kg; snatched birds in flight and took **smaller /
juvenile moa** and other birds — so its pressure falls on chicks and small moa,
not adult giants. This is the predator to reskin the eagle system to.

**Local bone hook (nice pop-up, optional):** the **Te Ahu a Turanga** moa bones,
found 2021 during the Manawatū–Tararua highway build and held at **Te Manawa**,
were dated to **at least 180 ka, possibly ~345 ka** — an *assemblage* (two moa
genera, an extinct predator, and the extinct North Island goose, with hatchling
and adult bones). The ~345 ka estimate sits almost on the Whakamaru eruption, and
the taphonomy (gully fills, tephra falls, dune ponds bury bone) is *why* we have
any of this record. A quiet, literal tie between the installation and the museum.

## Cultural grounding (shapes what we build now vs. later)

The river is the ancestral relationship of **Rangitāne o Manawatū**; **Te Āpiti**
(the gorge) is an ancestral passage, and moa are **taonga** (the Te Ahu a Turanga
bones were returned to the Ruahine maunga under tikanga). Rangitāne sit on Te
Manawa's board. So: **naming, narration, and which stories are told are for
co-design with mana whenua — not hard-coded by us.** What's safe to build now is
the *system*: geology, ecology, the buttons and timeline, with content/labels left
as replaceable hooks.
