# Te Manawa — Native Fauna Longlist, Scored

A longlist to cut down from, scored against the four criteria you set. Companion to
`TEMANAWA_ECOLOGY.md` and `TEMANAWA_ECOLOGY_COAST.md`. Recommendation at §3.

## Scoring

| Axis | Question | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Known** | How much do people know and love it today? | never heard of it | on the coinage |
| **Wonder** | If extinct, how arresting is it? | extant and ordinary | stops you in your tracks |
| **Local** | How baked into the Manawatū's story? | incidental | the district's own |
| **Span** | How long present across 1 Ma → today? | arrived <1,000 yr ago | there the whole way |

**Span in detail** — this is the criterion that does the cutting:

- **5** — lineage in New Zealand for millions of years, in this region across the whole
  window, surviving to human arrival or beyond
- **4** — as above, but habitat-restricted here (estuary-only, alpine, fast rivers)
- **3** — present through most of the window, or presence strongly climate-gated
- **2** — arrived within the last ~2 Ma; presence at 1 Ma genuinely uncertain
- **1** — **arrived in the last ~1,000 years. Disqualifying.**

> **Honest caveat.** Species-level identity over a million years is uncertain for
> nearly everything below. Moa aDNA does show some populations holding the same
> geographic location for **hundreds of thousands of years**, which is unusually
> reassuring — but for most taxa what persists across the window is the **ecological
> role**, not the exact species. Treat species IDs as provisional, as
> `TEMANAWA_RESEARCH.md` already does.

| **Mantell's moa** *Pachyornis geranoides* | 1 | 4 | 5 | 5 | Throughout the NI, in **drier forest and shrubland** — the downland and dune moa. aDNA: populations held the same location for hundreds of thousands of years. |

small) and two dry-country moa (one inland, one coastal).** That's a habitat-driven
distribution you get for free from the terrain, not from scripting.

## 2. What the fauna *does* — two functions worth simulating

Beyond looking right, two animals earn their place mechanically:

1. **Moa browse.** Moa were the dominant browsers for millions of years, and the
   divaricating architecture of shrubs like *Coprosma acerosa* (already on the coastal
   flora shortlist) is widely read as a response to it. `[BUILD]` Browsing pressure
   should shape **shrubland structure**, and it is the one browsing signal with a
   pre-human analogue — unlike the possum/deer/cattle collapse Esler documents, which
   has none.
2. **Kererū dispersal.** The only surviving disperser of large podocarp and tawa
   fruit. `[BUILD]` A plausible, cheap coupling between a bird and the plant model:
   large-fruited trees only recruit where kererū go.

## 3. Recommendation — a cast of 12

Ordered by build priority. Scores in brackets as **Known / Wonder / Local / Span**.

**Core simulation — the existing moa/predator loop**

1. **North Island giant moa** *Dinornis novaezealandiae* — 5/5/5/5 — wet forest
2. **Mantell's moa** *Pachyornis geranoides* — 1/4/5/5 — dry forest, shrubland, dunes
3. **Eyles' harrier** — 1/5/4/5 — apex; pressure falls on chicks and small moa
4. **Coastal moa** *Euryapteryx* — 2/4/5/5 — *third moa, only if the terrain already
   separates a coastal band*

**Ecological function — animals that change the plants**

5. **Kererū** — 5/1/4/5 — large-fruit dispersal
6. **North Island goose** *Cnemiornis gracilis* — 1/4/5/5 — open-ground grazer, and
   **it is in the Te Ahu a Tūranga bones downstairs**
7. **Finsch's duck** — 1/4/3/5 — open/shrubland grazer; abundant in cold phases

**Recognition and local resonance**

8. **Huia** — 5/5/5/5 — the highest-scoring animal on the list; Tararua/Ruahine forest
9. **Kiwi** (NI brown) — 5/2/3/5 — the recognition anchor
10. **Whēkau / laughing owl** — 2/5/3/5 — night, plus the bone-record story

**Habitat signatures — one each for river and coast**

11. **Tuna / longfin eel** — 5/2/5/5 — the river itself; Rangitāne taonga
12. **Katipō** — 5/2/5/4 — the dune, and an indicator of the pre-marram state

**Two more if the budget stretches, both for local resonance over recognition:**

- ***Powelliphanta traversi*** — 2/4/5/5 — the only animal on this list whose *entire
  world range* is the ground this installation is standing on
- **Ngutu parore / wrybill** — 3/3/5/4 — the estuary, and ~1% of the world's population

**Cut, with reasons:** Haast's eagle (wrong island); swamp harrier and pūkeko (too
recent — and wrong in a way visitors will assume is right); tuatara (recognition is
high but the Manawatū link is weak and it won't read at screen scale); long-tailed bat
(span 2); adzebill and mōho (high wonder, but they compete with better-scoring
flightless birds for the same visual slot); small forest birds (**one flock asset, not
five entities**).

## 4. To verify before committing assets

1. **Euryapteryx taxonomy** — *gravis* vs *curtus* is unsettled; the coastal
   southern-NI distribution is attached to *gravis*, which some authors sink into
   *curtus*. Check current NZ Birds Online / Checklist Committee treatment.
2. **The "extinct predator" in the Te Ahu a Tūranga assemblage** — `TEMANAWA_RESEARCH.md`
   records the assemblage as two moa genera, an extinct predator and the extinct NI
   goose. Confirming *which* predator would either lock in Eyles' harrier from local
   bone or open a second option. Worth asking Te Manawa directly.
3. **Species-level persistence at 345 ka** — the aDNA result on long-term population
   structure is encouraging but was established on late-Quaternary material. Check how
   far back it actually reaches before claiming these exact species on a 345 ka label.
4. **Which moa ate what** — coprolite and gizzard-content work exists and would let the
   browse mechanic be species-specific rather than generic.
