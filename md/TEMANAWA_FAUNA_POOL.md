# Te Manawa — Fauna Pool

**For the illustrator, and for deciding how far to go.** Companion to
`TEMANAWA_ECOLOGY_FAUNA.md` (Deep Dive 5) and `TEMANAWA_FAUNA.md` (the scored
longlist).

---

## 0. What changed

The sim is **highly stylised and not to scale** — a moa sprite stands for a group of
moa in an area, not an individual. So my earlier filter ("must read at sprite scale")
was the wrong test, and it wrongly excluded some of the best animals on the list.
**Huia is reinstated** and sits in the core set.

**The revised filter is two questions:**

1. **Is it recognisable and loved, or arresting if extinct?** — as a stylised icon, not
   a field-guide plate.
2. **Does it do a job?** — habitat marker, era marker, predator, prey, or ambient
   colour.

**A third question was added later** (`TEMANAWA_PLAN_V2.md` §0.1), and it applies to
every note in this document: **does it read from directly overhead?** The visual hooks
below were written from a field-guide viewpoint, and a few of them — narrow upright
silhouettes especially — are much weaker seen from above than from the side. Where a
hook depends on a profile, the illustrator should find the bird's-eye equivalent:
wing shape, crown pattern, back markings, and the shape a bird makes when it moves.

**One hard filter remains: Span.** Anything that arrived in the last ~1,000 years is
out on factual grounds, not aesthetic ones. That list is at §5 and it is
non-negotiable — the swamp harrier and pūkeko would both be wrong in a way visitors
would assume was right.

## 1. The core set — seven

> ✅ **Adopted.** These seven are the built cast — see `TEMANAWA_PLAN_V2.md` §5.1. The
> *Dinornis* **male** is built as a second sprite (the dimorphism pair), and **coastal
> moa** *Euryapteryx* is the conditional eighth. This section supersedes
> `TEMANAWA_ECOLOGY_FAUNA.md` §8 on the cast.

| # | Animal | Visual hook | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **NI giant moa** *Dinornis novaezealandiae* | **Female towers over the male** — extreme reverse dimorphism, two sprites from one species | tall forest; the headline |
| 2 | **Eyles' harrier / kērangi** | Goshawk build — compact wings, huge legs and talons, owl-like face | apex predator |
| 3 | **Mantell's moa** *Pachyornis geranoides* | Smallest NI moa, stocky | **cold-phase** marker |
| 4 | **Little bush moa** *Anomalopteryx didiformis* | Small, rounded, low-slung; 50–90 cm at the back | **warm-phase** marker |
| 5 | **North Island goose** *Cnemiornis gracilis* | Heavy flightless goose | open grazing; **in the local bones** |
| 6 | **Kererū** | Iridescent green-bronze, white waistcoat, heavy | recognition anchor; harrier prey; seed disperser |
| 7 | **Huia** | **The most extreme bill dimorphism of any bird** — female's long thin curve, male's short chisel | **the Manawatū's own bird**; hill forest |

**Huia's case, restated:** it is the only animal that scores maximum on all four of your
criteria. 646 skins taken in 1883 from the forest between the **Manawatū Gorge and
Akitio**; a **rāhui placed on the Tararua by the chiefs of the Manawatū and Wairarapa**;
last confirmed sighting in the Tararua, **28 December 1907**. Black with a white tail
tip, orange wattles, ivory bill.

> **Note for the illustrator — dimorphism is the best value on this list.** Two species
> give you a second sprite almost free, and in both cases the difference is *the*
> memorable fact about the animal: **Dinornis** (female far larger) and **huia**
> (completely different bill). Same trick, two payoffs.

## 2. The next six — best value if you want more

Ranked by what each adds that the core seven don't.

| Animal | Visual hook | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| **Adzebill** *Aptornis otidiformis* | **80 cm, up to 12 kg, massive skull with a long, heavily down-curved bill, thick neck, short robust legs — and wings so reduced they were not visible on the living bird.** A wingless crane with a pickaxe for a face | The strangest thing on the list. **A second predator**, and an open-country one — so it's a cold-phase actor |
| **Finsch's duck** *Chenonetta finschi* | Near-flightless, heavy-bodied grazing duck | **Confirmed Eyles' harrier prey.** One of the most abundant birds in pre-human NZ. Reinforces *cold is busier, not emptier* |
| **Kiwi** (NI brown) | Unmistakable at any level of abstraction | **The highest recognition of any native animal.** Nocturnal ambient |
| **Kākāpō** | Moss-green sphere, owl face, absurd and beloved | Enormous public affection; nocturnal forest herbivore; reads instantly even heavily stylised |
| **Whēkau / laughing owl** | **Prominent white facial disc rimmed in dark brown, yellow eyes, hooked yellow bill**, brown with buff-yellow streaks; 35–40 cm, ~600 g | Night. Plus the best meta-story on the list: **its roost deposits are a major source of the small-bone fossil record** — a bird that is part of why we know any of this |
| **Matuku / bittern** | The frozen vertical bill-up posture among reeds | The wetland's signature, in one silhouette |

## 4. Notes for the illustrator

**Dimorphism pairs — best value on the list.** *Dinornis* (female far larger than male)
and **huia** (completely different bill shape). Two sprites each, and in both cases the
pair *is* the memorable fact.

**The palette is mostly brown, green and grey.** Plan the accents deliberately, because
they're scarce. The genuine colour on this list:

| Colour | From |
|---|---|
| **Gold / orange** | pīngao (plant), kōwhai (plant), tīeke saddle, huia wattles, tōrea bill |
| **Crimson** | kākā underwing, rewarewa flowers (plant) |
| **Blue** | kōkako wattles, whio, *Naultinus* gecko |
| **Yellow / white** | hihi male, whēkau facial disc, tūī throat tufts |
| **Red** | katipō stripe |

**Era markers should be visually unambiguous.** The cold-phase set (Mantell's moa, NI
goose, Finsch's duck, adzebill) and the warm-phase set (little bush moa, kererū, huia,
kākāpō) should feel like **different casts**, not the same birds recoloured — because
that's the readout for the vegetation.

**Nocturnal set.** Kiwi, kākāpō, whēkau, ruru, pekapeka, wētā. If the sim ever renders
night, these five or six become a distinct second cast on the same map — a lot of
apparent depth for one lighting state.

**Cold is busier, not emptier.** The glacial map should carry *more* large grazing
birds than the interglacial — NI goose, Finsch's duck, Mantell's moa, with the adzebill
and kērangi both at home in open shrubland. Worth making sure the art supports that
rather than fighting it.

## 5. The hard no list

Not aesthetic judgements — factual ones. All of these arrived in the last ~1,000 years
or belong to the wrong island.

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| **Swamp harrier** | Arrived after Polynesian settlement opened the forest. **The raptor niche belongs to kērangi and kārearea.** |
| **Pūkeko** | <1,000 years. Ubiquitous today, absent then |
| **Silvereye / tauhou** | 1832 |
| **Spur-winged plover** | 1930s |
| **White-faced heron**, **royal spoonbill** | 1940s |
| **Welcome swallow** | First bred 1958 |
| **Black swan** | 1860s Australian introduction. The native swan (poūwa) is a different, extinct bird |
| **Haast's eagle, kea, SI takahē, *Megalapteryx*** | South Island only |
| **Long-tailed bat** | Split from Australian congeners **<2 Ma** — presence at the old end of the window is genuinely uncertain |

Half of these are what a general audience pictures when they picture New Zealand
wildlife. **The absences are as much a part of the story as the presences** — and worth
a line in the interpretation if there's ever room for one.
