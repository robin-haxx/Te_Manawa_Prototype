# Te Manawa — An Ecology-First Concept

**What this is:** a design derived *only* from the four ecology deep dives, written
without reference to `TEMANAWA_PLAN.md`, then compared against it at the end. A
counterfactual, not a replacement. §7 is the merge that probably wins.
The actual installation will have a heavily refined scope and not follow from this document. These are speculative notes to provide an alternative angle for details.

---

## 1. The story the ecology tells

The existing plan's takeaway is geological and excellent: **the river is older than the
mountains.** Working forward from the ecology instead, a different sentence falls out:

> ## Nothing here holds still.
> ## The forest is buried, drowned, ashed and frozen — and it comes back every time.
> ## The moa live through all of it.

Every one of the four dives ended up at the same structure without being pushed there:

| Dive | The cycle it found |
|---|---|
| **Coast** | Sand advances, buries the plants, the plain deflates to the water table, the turf starts again a few hundred metres inland. **~50 years per turn.** |
| **Wetland** | Uplift feeds sediment down the gorge, the river builds levées, the back-swamp ponds and **drowns its own forest**, peat forms, the ground subsides, the river avulses, repeat. |
| **Lowland forest** | Podocarps recruit **only after catastrophe**, then age as a single cohort while tawa fills in underneath, until the next catastrophe. |
| **Open country** | Forest in the interglacial, two-thirds open in the glacial, forest again. **Three full turns inside our window.** |

Four habitats, four independent literatures, one shape: **disturbance, collapse,
return.** And the *same organisms* come back each time — New Zealand palynologists were
the first anywhere to argue for micro-refugia precisely because the pollen shows local
survival rather than wholesale extinction and recolonisation.

That is a different installation from "look how the land was made." It's **"look what
survives being remade."**

## 2. Five findings that would change the design

### 2.1 Over this window, the biota changes far more than the topography

The ranges began rising ~3 Ma with most uplift in the last 1 Myr. Across 345 ka → 50 ka
they gain a few hundred metres on a range already 1,500 m high. Meanwhile the vegetation
goes **forest → two-thirds open → forest, three times.** At the Last Glacial Maximum,
mean forest tree pollen across sites is **15.1%**, tree ferns **0.7%**, grass **40.8%**,
and there is *no clear altitudinal zonation at all* from sea level to 800 m.

`[DESIGN]` The uplift is the *reason* for the landscape but it is not the *drama* at
this timescale. **Animate the vegetation; leave the mountains nearly still.** This is
also, conveniently, far cheaper — the current plan calls terrain morph "the hard, new
part," and ecology-first largely deletes it.

### 2.2 The rhythm is a wave, not an arrow

Tectonics accumulate; climate oscillates. At 345 ka → 50 ka the ~100 kyr glacial beat
is the dominant signal and you get **about three full breaths**. A timeline that reads
as a monotonic arrow of uplift misrepresents what the period actually felt like.

`[DESIGN]` The timeline should read as a **sine, not a bar** — three big breaths with
smaller events inside them.

### 2.3 Every disturbance runs on its own clock

The dives produced four disturbance regimes with genuinely different recovery times, all
measured, none invented:

| Disturbance | Recovery | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Gale / flood** | **years** | shingle-bed flora reset every flood; >70 species with "short occupancy" |
| **Ashfall** | **decades–centuries** | <10 yr defoliation → **~60 yr wetland expansion** → centuries to re-sort composition |
| **Sand burial** | **~50 years** | bare wet hollow → toetoe and cabbage tree, per Esler's plain sequence |
| **Glacial** | **tens of millennia** | the ~100 kyr cycle |

`[DESIGN]` This is the best teaching structure in the whole body of research, and it's
invisible in the current button set. **Four buttons, four clocks** — press them and
watch the landscape recover at four wildly different speeds. Deep time stops being an
abstraction and becomes something you *compare against*.

### 2.4 Two things never change, and both are visible

- **The river holds its course** (antecedence — the existing takeaway).
- **The wind holds its direction.** Koputaroa dune sand is mineralogically coastal, not
  fluvial, which means the prevailing north-westerly was blowing during the last glacial
  exactly as it blows now.

`[DESIGN]` The river constant is something you must be *told*. The wind constant is
**on screen every frame** — every dune in every era points the same way. Free coherence,
and a second anchor for the same idea.

### 2.5 There was no fire

No ignition source in this window. New Zealand's lightning-fire regime is negligible;
natural ignition was volcanic. Everything Esler describes as grassland, mānuka
shrubland and bracken is **post-1280 CE, roughly 700 years old.**

`[DESIGN]` **Cut fire.** It also means the open landscape has to be produced some other
way — and the LGM literature is explicit that it isn't mean temperature or CO₂ either.
It's **frost and wind**, which is a much more interesting mechanism and a much cheaper
one.

## 3. The installation this suggests

### 3.1 One place, three breaths

The screen shows a single stretch of the Manawatū — coast, plain, river, ranges —
across three glacial cycles. The landform is nearly constant. The **living cover is the
moving part**, and it changes completely, three times.

### 3.2 The four buttons become four disturbances at four timescales

| Button | Immediate — the instant you press it | Aftermath — if you stay |
|---|---|---|
| **Wind** | sand walks ESE; shingle bars scour to bare grey; emergents snap in the forest | pīngao and spinifex **grow into** the new sand; kahikatea seedlings take the fresh silt; the plain rebuilds over ~50 years |
| **Ash** | the canopy greys and thins; ash sheets the ground | wetlands **bloom green for 60 years**; peat thickens ~4×; the forest returns with a **different** species mix |
| **Cold** | frost pools in the basins and valley floors first, sparing sheltered slopes; **tree ferns vanish** | forest retreats to sheltered pockets; grey scrub and tussock spread; the coast walks 30 km west |
| **Growth** | a different result in **every** habitat | peat → raupō and harakeke; alluvium → kahikatea; sand → kānuka; **the dry NW downlands barely respond at all** |

Two things this does that the current set doesn't:

- **Every button does something different in every habitat.** That's the single most
  repeatable finding across all four dives, and it means one press teaches four things.
- **One button visibly fails somewhere.** Growth does almost nothing on the droughty
  Rongotea downlands, because there the limit is the <914 mm rainfall, not fertility. A
  button that doesn't always work teaches more than one that does.

### 3.3 Time is the default state, not a button

Ecology-first, **the installation is always running** and the buttons are perturbations
against a landscape already moving through its cycle. "Deep time" isn't a thing you
trigger; it's the water everything else swims in.

This is the sharpest disagreement with the current plan, and it has a real cost — see
§6.

### 3.4 Frost is the one new parameter, and it's cheap

A scalar off the glacial index, applied **topographically**: pooling in basins and
valley floors, sparing sheltered slopes. That single rule reproduces the patchy
forest–shrub–grassland mosaic the pollen records show, including the micro-refugia,
for almost no cost. Combined with the already-fixed NW wind, **one climate signal opens
the forest at one end of the map and builds the dunefield at the other.**

## 4. What the moa are for

In an ecology-first design the moa stop being the subject and become **the legend** —
how you read the vegetation without a single word of text.

| Species | Habitat | Peaks in |
|---|---|---|
| **Little bush moa** *Anomalopteryx didiformis* | closed wet forest | interglacial |
| **NI giant moa** *Dinornis novaezealandiae* | tall forest | interglacial |
| **Mantell's moa** *Pachyornis geranoides* | **drier forest and shrubland** | **glacial** |
| **Coastal moa** *Euryapteryx* | coast and dunes | follows the shoreline |

This is sourced habitat partitioning, not invented: *Dinornis* and *Anomalopteryx* in
high-rainfall forest, *Euryapteryx* and *Pachyornis* in drier forest and shrubland.

`[DESIGN]` **The cast changes as the climate swings.** You know a glacial is coming
because the little bush moa thins out and the shrubland moa multiplies. That is a far
better use of having four moa than four values of `preferredElevation` — and it makes
the vegetation legible to someone who can't tell a podocarp from a beech.

Two supporting cast members earn their place the same way: **Finsch's duck and the
North Island goose** are open-country grazers, so the **glacial map should be busier
with large grazing birds than the interglacial one, not emptier.** Cold ≠ dead. And
the harrier simply follows total prey biomass.

## 5. Ninety seconds, as a visitor

> A forested plain, a river braiding across it, dunes at the left edge marching inland.
> Moa move through the canopy gaps.
>
> **You press Wind.** The dune field jumps a step ESE; a shingle bar goes bare and grey;
> two tall trees fall. Within seconds the sand greens over — the grasses that just got
> buried are *growing*.
>
> **You press Ash.** The whole canopy goes grey-brown. It looks like a disaster. Then
> the swamps turn vivid green and stay that way; the forest comes back over the next
> while, and it isn't quite the forest that left.
>
> **You press Cold.** Frost settles into the low ground first. The tree ferns go. The
> forest pulls back into the gullies, grey scrub spreads across the terraces, the sea
> retreats and the dunes surge inland after it. The little bush moa is gone; there are
> more moa than before, but different ones, and geese with them.
>
> You wait. It warms. The forest walks back out of the gullies.
>
> **The dunes still point the same way. The river is still in the same gorge.**

That last line is the takeaway, and it lands without text.

## 6. What this version loses

Being honest, because these are real:

1. **"The river is older than the mountains."** It's the best single sentence in the
   project, it ties directly to **Te Āpiti** and to Rangitāne, and it is *geological*.
   Ecology-first buries it.
2. **The gorge-cutting visual** — the signature image, and the clearest tie to the
   sibling landform screen next door.
3. **Spectacle.** The Whakamaru flash is a better *hook for a passing visitor* than any
   ecological event. Ecology's best moments are slow.
4. **Fast-forward.** §3.3 argues time should be ambient — but a kiosk visitor gives you
   maybe 40 seconds, and a fast-forward button is the honest way to show a 100 kyr cycle
   in that budget. **This is the point where ecology-first is probably wrong.**

## 7. The merge — what I'd actually build

Keep the geological **frame**; let ecology drive the **animation** and the **buttons**.

**Keep from the current plan**

- The takeaway sentence and the Te Āpiti tie. It's the frame, the timeline label, the
  pop-up.
- **Deep time as Button 1.** The kiosk needs it, and ecology-first has no answer to a
  40-second attention span.
- The moa/kērangi emergent loop, the attract cycle, the co-design hooks, the
  photosensitivity and accessibility work.

**Change**

| Current | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain morph is "the hard, new part" | **Terrain nearly static; vegetation carries the change** | The biota changes far more than the topography over this window, and it's cheaper and more legible (§2.1) |
| Storm = stalls the predator ~20 s | **Storm = wind: dune advance, flood scour, gale damage — different in every habitat** | Dive 1 §10, Dive 2 §7. Distracting the harrier is the least interesting thing a storm does |
| Growth = generic productivity surge | **Growth = fertility, not growth rate** | Dive 2 §8. Different result in every habitat, including one where it correctly does almost nothing |
| Eruption = reset to 345 ka | **Eruption = five-stage sequence with a 60-year wetland bloom** | Dive 2 §6. An eruption *fertilises*; resetting to zero is both duller and wrong |
| Glacial index biases snowline/forest | **Add frost as a topographically selective event** | Dive 4 §4.3. Mean temperature doesn't explain the open landscape; frost and wind do |
| Four moa with different elevations | **Four moa as the legend for the vegetation** | §4. Makes the botany readable without text |
| — | **Fix wind direction NW for the entire run** | Dive 1 §1. Sourced, free, and visible every frame |
| — | **Cut fire as a routine process** | Dive 4 §3.3. No ignition source in this window |
| — | **Podocarps recruit only after disturbance** | Dive 3 §4. Couples the buttons to the forest instead of letting it grow uniformly |

**Add one thing**

A **"light at ground level"** derived field. It turned up independently in four
places — mānuka succession, swamp-forest understorey richness, podocarp gap-filling,
and post-eruption defoliation. One field, four systems, and it's what makes the
aftermath of every button legible.

**Net effect on the build phases:** Phase 3 (terrain morph) shrinks a lot; Phase 5
(ecology) and Phase 6 (buttons) grow. Given Phase 3 was the risky one, that's a
favourable trade.

## 8. Open questions this raises

1. **Does the timeline read as an arrow or a wave?** The geology says arrow, the ecology
   says wave. They can coexist — uplift as a thin monotonic band under an oscillating
   climate band — but it's a real design choice.
2. **Is "Cold" a fifth button, or is it what Deep Time shows you?** Folding frost into
   the fast-forward keeps four buttons but makes Deep Time do two jobs.
3. **Do the absences get acknowledged?** You've said the installation won't depict human
   occupation. But the *most* striking material in the research is what's missing now —
   97% of the wetland, under 5% of the plains vegetation, the rātā, the seed-dispersal
   network. A single quiet end-card would carry it without depicting the transition. Your
   call, and it's a curatorial one rather than a technical one.
4. **How much does the sibling landform screen constrain this?** If that screen already
   carries the geology, ecology-first here is *complementary* rather than competing —
   which would tilt the merge further toward ecology than §7 does.
